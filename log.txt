2026-10-20  Rough: move-up x command level; terrain batches 0.3-3, generator legs capped
  - Symptom: every rough run (06-23, 12-20, 13-16, 15-37) drops terrain
    level from 3.5 to ~0 by iteration ~250 and stays there (15-37 at
    iteration 1030: 0.15, though 65 % of episodes time out).
  - Diagnosis: commands are multiplied by the per-robot command level
    (start 0.5) but move-up stayed Go2's 4 m x speed scale. Simulated
    perfect tracker, flat, 20 s, random headings, ranges before step 5000:
    level 0.5 up 15 % / down 49 % (median 2.4 m), 0.66 up 34 % / down 45 %,
    1.0 up 62 % / down 31 %. The curriculum could only go down.
  - Fix: terrain_levels_vel move-up = half of the batch's scaled patch
    (4 m x terrain scale, was 4 m x sqrt(s_L)) x the robot's command level
    (move-down already uses the actual command). On the speed scale, x0.3
    robots had to cross ~2 of their 2.4 m patches and x3 robots moved up
    after 6.9 m of a 24 m patch. Perfect-tracker move-up odds before step
    5000 now 0.87 (x0.3) ... 0.62 (x1) ... 0.24 (x3), was 0.62 for all;
    after, 0.97 ... 0.89 ... 0.70. Unused terrain_scale param removed.
  - Terrain batches 0.15, 4 and 5 removed (TERRAIN_BATCH_SCALES 0.3-3, 7
    sims instead of 10). quadruped_generator redraws robots whose leg length
    is outside 0.225-3.5x Go2's (midpoints to the dropped batches; ~90 % of
    draws kept, scale still 1/4-4). mjcf_fix.TRAINING_SIZE_RANGE 0.24-3.37
    (kept p1-p99).
  - Checked: 300 robots from seed 1437162221 measure 0.229-3.49x, batches
    49/53/41/43/42/45/27. Not yet trained.

2026-10-20  Rough: backing boxes under thin terrain; Go2-unit curriculum; spawn offset scaled
  - Symptom: run 2026-10-20_12-20-33 (height-scan fix) tracked the previous
    run exactly (iteration 108: terrain_levels 0.499 vs 0.499, illegal
    contact 2.24 vs 2.22 per step).
  - Diagnosis (CPU rollouts of 2026-10-20_06-23-45/model_2000.pt, 28 robots
    per batch, row 0, 20 s): most robots never die (x0.3 23/28, x0.5 24/28,
    x1 26/28, x3 24/28); the illegal_contact flood comes from a few robots
    dying 0.08 s after every reset (~200 times per 20 s each), only on
    pyramid_stairs_inv, pyramid_stairs and waves. Replay of one (x0.5,
    0.7 kg-ish, 4 mm feet, "><" knees): its front feet sink 6 cm through the
    pit floor and the calves land; on the flat column it stands at 0.13 m.
    Ground thickness under the patch centre, row 0: inverted-stair pit floor
    0 mm (x0.3) / 0.6 mm (x0.5) / 7 mm (x1) - mjlab makes it a zero-height
    box, total_height - (num_steps + 1) * step_height - and wave hfield
    base 0.6-1.5 mm (x0.3-x0.5); flat is a 1 m box.
  - Fix 1: velocity_env_cfg._BackedTerrainCfg wraps every sub-terrain but
    flat with a box (patch size / 8 deep) whose top is the patch's lowest
    surface (box tops, hfield elevation 0). +160 geoms per sim (2124 ->
    2284). Rays over every patch at x0.5 / x1 / x3: the backing never rises
    above the surface (0.0000 mm). Same rollouts: x0.3 619 -> 2 illegal
    contacts (27/28 never die), x0.5 201 -> 2 (two robots still fall on
    waves / stairs ~0.7 s in, 37 times: the old policy, not the ground).
  - Fix 2: terrain_levels_vel move-up = half of Go2's patch (grid size /
    terrain_scale, set by scale_terrain) x the robot's speed scale, replacing
    "half a scaled patch". Speeds scale with sqrt(s_L) but the episode stays
    20 s, so half a scaled patch was ~2.2x Go2's effort for x5 (8.9 Go2
    seconds) and too easy for x0.15. Single grid at 1.0: unchanged.
  - Fix 3: scale_terrain scales reset_base's +-0.5 m spawn offset by S. CPU
    spawn check: unscaled, 23-26% of x0.15 spawns on rows 2-9 started on
    ground > 0.05 S above the origin (into a slope or stair); x0.3+ were fine.
  - Checked: train.py --task rough on CPU (96 envs, 10 sims, 2 iterations).

2026-10-20  Height scan scaled per terrain batch; foot clearance from the ground under the foot
  - What: velocity_env_cfg.py scale_terrain also scales the terrain_scan ray
    grid (same 17 x 11 rays, size and spacing x S: denser for small robots,
    sparser for large), max_distance, the 1/max_distance normalisation and
    the +-0.1 m noise (added in metres, before scaling). terrain_scan sees
    the terrain only (include_geom_groups=(0,)). env_cfgs.py adds one
    downward ray per foot (foot_ray_<site>, terrain only, starting 1 m above
    the foot); mdp.foot_heights_above_ground gives foot height above the
    ground under it, used by foot_clearance, feet_swing_height and the
    critic's foot_height. Flat drops the foot rays (heights above z = 0).
  - Why: run 2026-10-20_06-23-45 (2048 envs, 10 terrain batches, stopped at
    2000): terrain_levels 3.5 -> 0.05 by iteration 1000 and stuck, episodes
    ~100 of 1000 steps, illegal_contact ~6x time_out; only the x1 robots
    walked in play. Reward terms are the same on flat and rough (flat is
    rough minus terrain, height scan and terrain curriculum), but two
    rough-only parts were in Go2 metres: the height scan (1.6 x 1.0 m at
    0.1 m: an x0.15 robot's 4.5 cm steps fell between rays and a 1.5 cm step
    was 0.003 of the observation; an x5 robot saw less than its body) and
    foot_clearance (foot z above z = 0, so a 0.3 m pyramid top at x1 counted
    as 0.3 m of clearance). 5 of 187 scan rays also hit the robot's own hip
    links (visual geoms are group 2).
  - Checked: GPU terrain_scan matches MuJoCo's CPU mj_ray exactly (all 187
    rays). Foot rays first hit the calf's visual capsule (the foot site sits
    inside it) and, for small robots sunk into a stair box, started inside the
    box and missed it; both fixed (terrain only, raised start). Robots s_L
    0.3 / 1 / 3 on x0.3 / x1 / x3 stairs: actor obs 238 in every batch,
    normalised scan ~0.05-0.06 for all, foot height above ground 0-5 mm /
    0-17 mm / 19-51 mm (foot radius minus sinking); flat unchanged.
    train.py --task rough (512 envs, 3 it) and --task flat (128, 2 it) run.

2026-10-18  Rough terrain scaled per env to each robot (replaces the size bins) (e2f7e39)
  - What: tasks/velocity/terrain.py rewritten. The grid is mjlab's
    ROUGH_TERRAINS_CFG at Go2 scale, 10 rows x 7 cols (one column per type,
    8 m tiles). Startup event scale_terrain (after morphology) scales the
    whole grid in each world by the robot's s_L, snapped to 24 log-spaced
    scales over 0.15-5 (neighbours 16% apart): box geoms get geom_size /
    rbound / aabb / pos x s; heightfield tiles are compiled 24 times with the
    same heights and the hfield size baked per scale (_ScaledCopies), and per
    world only the copy of its scale stays in place, the rest are parked
    1000 m down. Env origins = terrain origin x s (scale_env_origins, also
    used by terrain_levels_vel). Types are shared out evenly over envs and
    over sizes (envs sorted by s_L take the types in turn). Play's reset event
    picks a random row/type. vis_rough.py: --bin removed; the viewer copies
    env 0's scaled terrain into the CPU model.
  - Why: with bins, robots near a bin edge got terrain up to ~1.5x off their
    size, and a robot walking off its tile landed on another bin's tile.
    Now every tile in a world is that robot's size (within 8%), so leaving
    the tile is harmless. mujoco_warp keeps one hfield size per hfield (not
    per world), hence the per-scale copies; terrain geoms are static, so
    their world positions (data.geom_xpos, set once in make_data) are
    written directly.
  - Cost: ngeom 2660 -> 1514 (960 hfield copies, 1474 terrain geoms).
  - Checked: scratch script, 14 robots s_L 0.17-3.19: 2 envs per type, each
    world has exactly 40 hfield geoms in place (4 hf types x 10 rows); after
    2 s of zero actions every base sits 0.22-0.28 s_L above its origin with
    feet in contact (before the copy-position fix the hf-type robots fell
    through). train_test.py --task rough, 8 robots, 3 s: no crash/NaN.
    vis_rough.py --viewer False runs.

2026-10-12  Morphology-general rewards and training (d9b9c70, fd3589f,
            5bd09c9, 5b6fd25, c06aaef, 60c7f8b)
  Why: the policy must work on any quadruped of this shape. Measured on 200
  generated robots (seed 1, p5-p95): leg length 0.57-1.61x Go2's, mass
  0.40-1.53x, torque scale (s_m * s_L) 0.27-2.06x. A term not expressed in
  Go2 units or as a ratio to the robot's own size/limits pulls several times
  harder on some bodies than others, so the policy over-fits whichever
  bodies dominate the loss. Every change below makes a term mean the same on
  every robot, or lets each robot learn at its own pace.

  1. Size-corrected existing terms (d9b9c70)
     - joint_acc_l2 -> scaled_joint_acc_l2, x s_L^2. Joint acceleration
       ~ 1/s_L, so the raw penalty was ~8x apart across the bank.
     - body_ang_vel x s_L (angular rate^2 ~ 1/s_L).
     - foot_clearance / s_L^1.5 (height error ~ s_L times foot speed
       ~ s_L^0.5); only its target was scaled before.
     - Yaw everywhere: command range, heading-control stiffness and clip,
       track_angular_velocity std, angular pushes and the joystick now use
       angular rate ~ 1/sqrt(s_L). Before, big robots were asked to turn
       relatively harder than small ones. normalized_command also returns yaw
       in Go2 units, so all command-gated terms compare like with like.
     Checked: train_test.py runs; it now also prints each robot's yaw range.

  2. base_height, -5.0 (fd3589f)
     The bank now stores each robot's base height above its feet in the
     default pose (base_above_feet). Penalty = squared fractional error of the
     base above the mean of the feet in contact; zero in flight.
     Why: the pose term alone allows crouched / kneeling gaits on some bodies
     (train_test.py notes robots sagging onto their calves). Measured against
     the feet, not the floor, so it holds on rough terrain; a fraction, so it
     means the same for every size.
     Checked: zero-action robots that sag get ~-0.16 per step.

  3. mechanical_power, -0.5 (5bd09c9)
     sum max(tau * qdot, 0) / (m g sqrt(g L)).
     Why: torque^2 needs per-size tuning; Froude power is dimensionless, so
     one weight fits every robot and it rewards efficient gaits. Not cost of
     transport (divides by speed, blows up when standing).
     Bug avoided: actuator_force is in actuator order (hips, thighs, calves),
     not joint order; _joint_order_torques reorders before multiplying by
     joint speed. Checked by printing actuator target_ids vs ctrl ids.

  4. actuator_headroom, -1.0 (5bd09c9)
     Squared excess of requested PD torque over 80% of the torque the motor
     can give at its current speed (its DC torque-speed band), and of joint
     speed over 90% of its limit.
     Why: each robot has different limits, so only a ratio works for all.
     Saturation is where behaviour transfers worst to unseen bodies; the
     requested (pre-clip) torque keeps commands beyond the band visible
     (KAIST Hound Sec. III-D).
     Checked: forced joint-target offsets of 0 / 0.3 / 0.8 / 1.5 rad give
     0 / 0 / 0 / 2.96; random actions stay at 0 (about 20% of the band).

  5. nonfoot_contact, -1.0 (5b6fd25)
     Non-foot ground force / illegal_contact threshold (10 N Go2, x s_m),
     capped at 1 per geom.
     Why: illegal_contact only terminates, one sudden punishment. A graded
     penalty below the threshold helps long-legged and low-slung bodies
     learn to keep knees and thighs off the ground.

  6. stance_width, -10.0 (5b6fd25)
     In the base frame, each foot should stay at least half its hip's
     lateral offset out from the midline; the shortfall / leg length,
     squared. Hip and foot lists use preserve_order so legs pair up.
     Why: hip width varies across generated robots; narrow ones tend to
     cross or tangle their legs.

  7. feet_air_time +0.5 (0.3 s Go2, time-scaled); foot_gait tapered
     0.5 -> 0.25 at step 5000*24 (5b6fd25)
     Why: foot_gait imposes one fixed clock period; free-cadence air time
     lets each body settle on its own stepping rhythm.
     Checked (5-7): all run on flat and rough terrain.

  8. Reward per robot size (c06aaef, window fixed in 60c7f8b)
     Episode returns split into leg-length thirds, logged as
     Morphology/mean_reward_{small,medium,large} and drawn by --plot (CSV gets
     three more columns).
     Why: shows if the policy fits one body size at the others' expense.
     Fix: each third first kept its own last 100 episodes, so they reached
     further back than rsl_rl's overall mean (the overall line fell below all
     three). Now one shared last-100 window; overall sits between the thirds.

  9. Per-robot command curriculum, command_levels (c06aaef)
     Each robot's sampled commands (and heading yaw limit) are multiplied by
     its own level in [0.2, 1], 0.5 at start (1.0 in play). At each episode
     end: +0.1 if mean xy tracking error < 0.25 m/s (Go2 units) and no fall,
     -0.1 if > 0.5 m/s or it fell; episodes under 1 s are skipped.
     Why: the global velocity stages move every robot at once, so small or
     awkward bodies get commands they can't follow and stop learning.
     Checked: a robot tracking well for 2 s rose 0.5 -> 0.6; one that fell
     dropped to 0.4.

  10. Left-right mirror symmetry, train.py --symmetry (default True) (60c7f8b)
     tasks/velocity/rl/symmetry.py mirrors every observation term and the
     actions (FR<->FL, RR<->RL, abduction joints and lateral axes negated,
     trot clock shifted half a period, height-scan rows reversed); rsl_rl
     trains on both copies. Unknown observation terms raise a KeyError.
     Why: the generated robots are left-right symmetric, so mirrored samples
     are valid; doubles data and gives a symmetric gait, usually stronger
     than any symmetry reward.
     Checked: mirror applied twice is exact (actor, critic, actions); the
     residual self-mirror difference in the default pose matches observation
     noise.

  End-to-end check: 64 robots, 12-15 iterations with --plot: all terms
  log, symmetry loss ~0.002, command level and per-size curves update. Too
  short to judge learning; next step is a full run compared against
  2026-10-12_14-21-18 (started on the 68e8a31 reward set, before this batch).

2026-10-12  KAIST Hound reward terms (68e8a31)
  Source paper
    Shin, Song, Ji, Park, "Actuator-Constrained Reinforcement Learning for
    High-Speed Quadrupedal Locomotion", arXiv 2312.17507 (Dec 2023). KAIST
    Hound, 45 kg, runs 6.5 m/s on a treadmill. Three ingredients:
      1. Motor operating region (MOR, Sec. II-A..C): joint torque is mapped
         to motor space and clipped to the linear torque-speed band
         -V_bus <= (R/Kt) tau + omega/Kv <= V_bus, capped at tau_peak. The
         band is wider in the braking (2nd/4th) quadrants.
      2. Reward terms (Sec. II-D/E, Table I), notably a contact velocity
         penalty and a diagonal-pair gait reward.
      3. Curriculum (Sec. II-E.3): forward command widened from
         U(-0.3, 1.5) to U(-1.4, 7.0) m/s, with the tracking weights raised
         as speed grows.
    Real-robot ablation (Table II), top speed: full method 6.5 m/s, without
    gait reward 6.0, without the light foot 5.5, without MOR 4.5.

  Paper -> this project
    MOR clipping: ALREADY PRESENT. mjlab's DcMotorActuator (_clip_effort)
      applies the same linear torque-speed band, wider in the braking
      quadrants, scaled per robot by tasks/velocity/mdp/morphology.py. No
      change. Open question: saturation_effort == effort_limit in
      assets/morph_quadruped/constants.py leaves no constant-torque region at
      low speed; raise saturation_effort if the real motors allow it.
    Weight convention: paper weights are relative to C_V = 6, ours to
      track_linear_velocity = 1, so paper weights were divided by 6. Every new
      term is normalised to Go2 units by Froude scaling (scaling.py) so robot
      size does not change its pull.

  Added terms (tasks/velocity/mdp/rewards.py, velocity_env_cfg.py)
    - foot_contact_velocity, weight -0.5   [paper: C_contact = -6.0]
        sum over feet of v_z^2 in the step before first contact, / s_L.
        Paper adds it because RaiSim's hard contacts don't model the bounce
        (restitution) of a fast touchdown. MuJoCo's soft contacts make that
        reason weaker, but pre-impact speed is a cleaner signal than the
        touchdown force soft_landing samples at 50 Hz.
    - diagonal_gait, weight +0.2   [paper: C_gait = 1.2]
        1 when exactly one diagonal pair (FR+RL or FL+RR) is in stance, no
        clock. Paper: without it motor power biased to one side and a single
        saturated motor capped speed (6.0 vs 6.5 m/s). Gated to commands
        above 1.5 m/s (Go2 units) so the clocked foot_gait still shapes
        walking; foot_gait's fixed 0.6 s period caps stride rate at speed.
    - joint_torques_l2 (scaled_joint_torques_l2), weight -1e-4
        [paper: C_tau = -2e-4 with C_V = 6]. Was missing. Torque divided by
        s_m * s_L. Weight is an estimate: Hound's torques are far larger than
        a Go2's, so the paper's ratio was not copied directly.
    - joint_vel_l2 (scaled_joint_vel_l2), weight -5e-5
        [paper: C_qdot = -3e-4 -> /6]. Was missing. Squared speed * s_L.
    - action_acc_l2, weight -0.01   [paper: C_smoothness2 = -0.8]
        Second difference of the actions. Paper Sec. III-D / Fig. 11: a torque
        command beyond the MOR is clipped, so the torque penalty never sees
        it; the jerky action needed to produce it is what the smoothness
        terms penalise, and that is how the policy learns to stay inside the
        motor band (Fig. 12: fewer MOR violations). Without smoothness terms
        their real robot vibrated.

  Changed weights
    - soft_landing -1e-3 -> -5e-4: impacts now mostly covered by
      foot_contact_velocity.
    - track_linear_velocity 1.0 -> 2.0 and track_angular_velocity 1.0 -> 1.5
      at step 5000*24, alongside the command-range widening (reward_weight
      curriculum). Paper: as speed rises, the effort penalties grow and the
      policy abandons fast commands unless tracking weights rise too.

  Compared and left unchanged
    - action_rate_l2 -0.05 [paper C_smoothness1 = -2.5 on desired joint
      positions]: with our 0.25 action scale it is already as strong
      relative to tracking.
    - joint_acc_l2 -2.5e-7 [paper C_qddot = -6.7e-3 on per-step velocity
      change]: same order after converting to rad/s^2.
    - foot_slip, foot_clearance, body_orientation_l2, body_ang_vel /
      angular_momentum (paper's base motion term), pose (paper's joint
      position term): equivalents already present.
    - foot_gait, stand_still, is_terminated: walking/standing shaping the
      paper does not cover.

  Not adopted (not reward changes)
    - Lightweight foot redesign (hardware).
    - Motor current-torque nonlinearity compensation (real-robot only).
    - Episode start command ramp / carrying the last state into the next
      episode (Sec. II-E.3): candidates if robots stall on large command
      jumps.

  Checked: train_test.py headless run loads all 20 reward terms and the 3
    curriculum terms without errors. Not yet trained; tuning notes in
    CLAUDE.md (halve joint_torques_l2 first if low-speed tracking stalls).
